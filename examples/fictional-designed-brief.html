RelayDesk Handoff Health Check

1. Executive Summary Check ticket-transfer summaries before handoff so receiving agents can continue without reconstructing the case. The need is plausible but unverified. Validate the problem and a simple checklist before investing in AI.
Problem framing
2. Problem

Under time pressure, agents may omit goals, actions taken, unresolved questions, or commitments. Receiving agents then reread threads or ask customers to repeat context. Frequency and severity are assumptions.

3. Opportunity

A transfer-time check can catch gaps while the sender can still repair them. Strategic fit is credible inside RelayDesk, but demand, incidence, and willingness to adopt remain unknown.

4. Proposed Solution

Show a four-item completeness check before transfer; point to unclear context; let agents edit, override, or mark items not applicable. Do not rewrite or block transfers by default.

Audience & advantage
5. Target User

Support agents transferring multi-step tickets. Their desired outcome: the next agent can identify and take the next action without rebuilding case history.

6. Why This Will Win

Workflow placement and existing ticket context are plausible advantages. Templates or required fields may solve the problem more cheaply; meaningful competitive differentiation is unverified.

7. Business Impact

Potentially lowers handling cost and improves continuity. Costs include review time, latency, false warnings, inference, and implementation. No baseline supports quantification.

Test & decide
8. Success Metrics

Primary: transfers where the receiver can identify the next action without reopening the thread. Track reconstruction time and repeat questions; guard added transfer time and false warnings.

9. MVP & Scope

Run a two-week manual review, then test a no-AI clickable checklist with 8–12 agents. Exclude rewriting, transfer blocking, multilingual checks, and production model integration.

10. Key Risks & Assumptions

Critical assumption: incomplete summaries frequently cause poor handoffs. Review a representative sample and stop if gaps are uncommon. Human override remains mandatory.

11. Recommendation
Proceed with Validation

Do not approve production development. First prove that missing context is common, the rubric predicts easier continuation, and agents tolerate the added step. If most handoffs are already complete, change the decision to Do Not Proceed. Supporting analysis and sources → illustrative-one-pager.md
